What this is about

When a property is sold at a tax sale or a foreclosure auction, it sometimes sells for more than what was owed.

The extra money does not belong to the county or the bank. It belongs to the former owner. Until you claim it, it is unclaimed money the county is holding for you. The county will call it surplus funds, excess proceeds, or an overage, so use those words when you call.

Most people are never told clearly that this money exists. It sits with the county until someone claims it, and there is almost always a deadline.

My work is finding former owners who have money sitting there and letting them know.

If the owner was a family member who has passed away

The money does not disappear when the owner dies. It generally passes to their heirs. If the property belonged to your parent, your spouse, or another relative who has died, you may be the person owed.

Claiming as an heir takes more proof, and the rules differ from state to state. Ask the county what they need from an heir when you call.

Check it yourself first

  1. Look up the phone number for the county where the property was. Find the number on the county's own website. Do not use a number I gave you. If you are not sure which office to call, call the main county number and ask who holds surplus funds from property sales.
  2. Give them the address of your old property and ask: "Are there surplus funds from the sale of this property?"
  3. Ask how much, and ask how a former owner files a claim.

That call costs you nothing. If they say there is no money, then I was wrong, and you have lost five minutes.

This is usually time sensitive

In most cases there is a clock on this money, and each state sets its own.

By the time you read this, it may already be too late to claim this money. I am not saying that to rush you. I am saying it because it is true, and because the fix is the same phone call: when you call the county, ask what the deadline is for your claim.

What happens after the deadline depends on the state. In some states the money moves to the state's unclaimed property office, and you may still be able to claim it there. In others, the right to it can be lost for good.
